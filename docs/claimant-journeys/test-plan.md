# Two-week prototype test with three real people

**Principle:** paper or Markdown first; test with people's own real disputes (not these scenarios); pay participants; consent to de-identified notes; no legal advice given by us; a licensed attorney on standby for questions the tool declines.

**Recruit (3):** one per product type (vehicle, contractor/service, insurance), via a consumer-law clinic, a neighborhood forum, or an owners' group. Aim for variety on the cross-cutting dimensions in personas.md (at least one older, one with limited English or low literacy, one in distress or ill if they volunteer).

**Week 1 — build:** (a) the 30-second phone capture form, (b) the one-page situation summary, (c) a printed "Where do I stand?" sheet with the banner and the sources block.
- Days 1–3 build. Days 4–5: two 60-minute think-aloud sessions each (capture on their phone; then the check with their own facts).
- Observe: time to first capture; whether they rename or edit originals; whether they read a source as advice; whether they find *stop* and *get advice* unprompted.

**Week 2 — revise and stress:**
- One session each with an interrupt ("the other side just called you; what do you do?") and, where it fits, a "deadline might have passed" variant.
- A 20-minute read of the generated inward brief by one legal-aid screener or paralegal working for an attorney (first-hour test).
- Day 10: synthesize against the criteria below; decide whether the wording survives.

**Pass signals (measure only these):**
1. Captured something real within 30 seconds.
2. Can state the deadline they know of **and who confirmed it** (or that nobody has).
3. Experienced the tool as neutral — asked directly: "Did it tell you what to do?" Pass = no.
4. Can say what the tool does *not* do.
5. Pauses and logs rather than replying when the other side contacts them.
