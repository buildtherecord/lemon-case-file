# "Where do I stand?" check and handoffs

**What this is:** plain questions that organize a claimant's situation and show what a qualified person would want to know. It does not decide anything, does not judge strength, does not predict outcomes, does not compute whether a deadline has passed, and never tells the claimant whether to file. It shows what to ask and where people can get advice about their situation.

**Rename note:** previously "Should I file?" — renamed so the tool is not framed as the decider.

## Required first inputs
Asked before the check shows anything, and before anything can be shared. (The first screen of the app is a capture; see `intake-and-ux.md`.)
- **State or jurisdiction** (required; every output depends on it).
- **Any lawyer already involved?**
- **Has anything been sent to or signed with the other side?**

## Always-on banner (every claimant screen with a path or a date)
This is for the claimant's own screens: the wizard and the inward record. The outward page has no banner of its own; `safety-gates.md` says why.
> Deadlines may apply that you and this tool don't know about. Stopping or waiting does not stop a deadline and may give up rights. Ask someone who can advise you about your situation.

## Questions
1. Is there a deadline you know of — on a letter, a contract, a policy, a notice? Where is it written, and who told you? (Entered by you. The tool does not calculate or confirm deadlines.)
2. What do you want? (Money back / replacement / a repair / a correction / an acknowledgment / to stop the problem.)
3. What amount is involved?
4. What do you have? (Documents / recordings / messages / only your memory.) You mark each one; the tool does not rate it.
5. What has the other side said in writing?
6. Has any offer, release, or agreement been put in front of you?
7. Who else is affected, a co-owner, or might be asked to speak about this?
8. Are you worried about how the other side may react?

## Output (same shape every time)
- Your situation on one page, in your words and your documents.
- Questions a lawyer will probably ask that you have not yet answered.
- **A generic list of routes people commonly use**, without "what it takes" or "likely cost": internal appeal or company review; arbitration or dispute program if one is offered; regulator complaint; mediation; chargeback or licensing board where relevant; demand letter; small claims; court; stopping and archiving. Some laws make the other side pay legal fees if you win, so cost can differ from what you expect.
- **Who can give advice about your situation:** legal aid organizations (some limit by income or case type), the state bar's lawyer referral service, courthouse self-help centers, attorneys who offer a first consultation (check how they are paid), and your state's consumer-protection or insurance office (complaints, not legal advice). The tool lists sources and the date each link was checked.
- **Support:** 988 (call or text) if you are in distress; peer-support helplines; low-cost counseling directories.
- The line: "This tool cannot tell you whether to file, whether you are in time, or what the law means for you."

## Guardrails
- No outcome predictions, scores, or "strong/weak" labels shown to the claimant. (The inward record's brief, which the attorney reads, shows only the *claimant's own* marks.)
- Statutes and policies shown as written, linked, with a verified-on date; no side-by-side readings generated by the tool. The claimant may write their own reading.
- Deadlines: claimant-entered only; all shown, none ranked as "safe"; the absence of a deadline never reads as "no deadline."
- Information about recording-consent rules and discovery appears before any recording or sharing feature: assume anything written or recorded may be demanded in a lawsuit; a record built before hiring a lawyer is generally not privileged; never delete anything.
- Before any share or export to a reviewer, journalist, or public page, which is to say before anything goes on the outward page: a gate that asks about confidentiality terms, naming of people or businesses, and whether an attorney has seen it.
- "Held back" means *not shared yet*; the tool never advises hiding or deleting evidence.
- Human review of wording by a licensed attorney in the user's state before release; corrections log visible.
