# Personas (patterns, not people)

Each persona is a **pattern** seen in consumer disputes, written as a composite. Nothing here is a real party's data. Variety is deliberate: no persona set should resemble any one real case. Tag: **[P]** = pattern widely reported; **[C]** = composite built for contrast.

## Claimants — by product

### Vehicle (repurchase / defect)
| ID | Pattern | What makes it different |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Used-car buyer, dealer sale "as is," transmission fails in week three [P] | Little statutory help; evidence is the sale paperwork and what was said; time pressure is cash |
| V2 | Retiree with a new gas pickup, many dealer visits for one stubborn noise [P] | Low tech comfort; adult child helps; faults are intermittent, hard to film |
| V3 | Gig driver whose leased car is the livelihood [C] | Income loss; lease terms; cannot be without the car for weeks |
| V4 | RV or motorhome owner, 14 months in, defects in the living portion [P] | Two warranties (chassis vs coach); out-of-state dealer; long repair queues |
| V5 | Motorcycle owner, safety defect, dealer closed or changed hands [C] | Counterparty has changed; recall status matters |
| V6 | Owner of a new EV with repeated electrical faults and open recalls [P] | Software faults; many visits; manufacturer review channel |

### Contractor / service dispute
| ID | Pattern | What makes it different |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Homeowner, large deposit, work never started [P] | Contract terms; licensing/registration; entity may disappear |
| S2 | Renter, security-deposit withholding with a "damages" list [P] | Statutory deadlines on landlord; photos at move-in/out; fear of eviction or bad reference |
| S3 | Car-repair customer, bill doubled after "found something else" [P] | Written estimate rules; vehicle held until paid |
| S4 | Couple, wedding vendor vanished a month before the date [P] | Emotion and urgency; chargeback window; vendor social media |
| S5 | Senior customer, moving company holding goods for ransom [P] | Possession of belongings; federal and state moving rules; coercion |
| S6 | Student, tuition paid to a program that closed [C] | Institutional failure; many similarly harmed; tuition recovery programs |

### Insurance denial / underpayment
| ID | Pattern | What makes it different |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | Employer-plan member, denied specialist treatment as "not medically necessary" [P] | Plan type decides the whole path (employer plan vs other); internal appeal then external review; short deadlines |
| I2 | Homeowner, hail-damaged roof, partial payment [P] | Contractor involvement; estimates differ; matching and depreciation language |
| I3 | Driver, car declared a total loss, offer far below replacement cost [P] | Comparable-vehicle valuation; appraisal clause; rental running out |
| I4 | Long-term disability claimant, benefit terminated after "surveillance" [P] | Medical records; plan documents; very short appeal windows; fatigue and illness |
| I5 | Traveler, trip cancelled for family illness, refund refused [C] | Small amount; many policy exclusions; credit-card coverage may help |
| I6 | Patient with an out-of-network surprise bill [P] | Billing codes; no-surprises rules; negotiation scripts |

### Money and authority (estates, agents, shared accounts)
| ID | Pattern | What makes it different |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Partner of an heir, organizing a parent's bank papers after a death; an agent under a power of attorney moved money [P] | The person building the record is not the rights holder; transactions must be placed against the authority's dates; the record accuses a named person, so it stays inward; families can be on both sides |
| M2 | Adult child who finds a co-signer drained a joint account [C] | Account ownership decides what the documents can show; bank records belong to the account holder |
| M3 | Beneficiary waiting on a trustee who will not account [C] | Records sit with the other side; the ask is an accounting, not a number |

### Cross-cutting claimant dimensions (assign to any persona to stress-test)
Language other than English · low literacy · no smartphone · rural, bad connectivity · disability · in crisis or ill · acting for a parent (proxy) · co-owner or co-signer · a small business, not a person · fear of retaliation · prior lawyer · already received a settlement offer · deadline possibly already passed

## Professionals and neutrals

| Role | First-hour need |
|---|---|
| Consumer attorney | Decide in minutes whether to take it: deadlines the *client* knows, dollar range, documents, conflicts, any prior lawyer, what has already been sent to the other side |
| Paralegal | Provenance, naming, gaps, clean export, exhibit numbers |
| Legal-aid intake screener | Eligibility first: income, county, claim type; the evidence comes second |
| Small-claims clerk / self-help center staff | Is this the right court; what forms; cannot give legal advice |
| Arbitration-program or company reviewer | A short, verifiable file and a specific ask |
| Regulator staff (AG, insurance department) | Was the complaint complete and specific enough to route |
| Journalist | Verifiable summary and permission to quote — and will find the weakest fact first |
| Volunteer coach | What may I say and not say; where is the line between information and advice |

Who sees which page: the other side's reviewer, neutrals, regulator staff, journalists and family read the **outward** page. Only the claimant and the claimant's attorney (including the paralegal or intake screener working for that attorney) read the **inward** record. There is no page in between.
