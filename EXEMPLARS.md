# Exemplary cases worth reading

The demo case in this repo (`docs/`) is **fictional** — every name, document, and
image is invented. We deliberately did not rebuild a real family's case file with
placeholder "evidence": fabricated exhibits attached to real parties would be
exactly the kind of unverifiable material this method exists to avoid.

The fictional fact pattern is modeled on denial patterns from real, published
appellate opinions that every consumer-warranty lawyer knows. If you want to see
how manufacturers actually fight these cases — and what records won anyway — read
the opinions themselves. They are public record and short.

## The reading list (California's Song-Beverly Act line)

- **Oregel v. American Isuzu Motors, Inc.** (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 1094.
  An oil leak the dealer could not locate across repeated visits; the manufacturer
  denied a defect existed and argued the owner hadn't proven one. The court: the
  owner's contemporaneous reports and the repair history were enough, and the
  refusal supported a civil penalty. The lesson this repo encodes: *"could not
  verify" visits are evidence for you, not against you — if you documented what
  you reported and when.*
  Opinion: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/90/1094.html

- **Jensen v. BMW of North America, Inc.** (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 112.
  A leading opinion on the scope of repurchase remedies and the restitution
  calculation. The lesson: *know the refund formula and the offset before any
  offer arrives* (see `templates/finance-worksheet.md`).
  Opinion: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/35/112.html

- **Kwan v. Mercedes-Benz of North America, Inc.** (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 174.
  On when a manufacturer's failure to repurchase is "willful" and exposes it to a
  civil penalty. The lesson: *a written demand, a complete record delivered to
  them, and their documented non-response change the stakes of saying no.*
  Opinion: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/23/174.html

These are California cases because that line of authority is the most developed
and the most cited nationally; your jurisdiction's standard will differ in detail
(contributions of jurisdiction notes welcome — see `CONTRIBUTING.md`).

## The demo case

`docs/index.html` (published via GitHub Pages) shows what a finished case file
looks like when the method is followed: the abstract, the plain-language record,
the component ledger with a recurrence column, the timeline, the evidence grid,
and the per-problem diagnostic-records request — for the fictional 2024 Acme
Meridian EV of a fictional owner, against a fictional manufacturer that "could
not verify" its way through two years of reports. Any resemblance to real
persons, vehicles, or manufacturers is unintended.
