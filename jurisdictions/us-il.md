## Repurchase / lemon standard

**New Vehicle Buyer Protection Act, 815 ILCS 380.** Illinois's lemon law for new
vehicles: repurchase or replacement where a nonconformity substantially impairs
use, value, or safety and is not repaired after a reasonable number of attempts
or days out of service. Read the current text for the presumption thresholds,
covered-vehicle definitions, and the statutory notice — the Act's numbers belong
in your record only as quotes from the current statute.

The federal **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act** and UCC warranty claims run alongside
the state act; how they relate to its time limits is counsel territory —
signpost only.

## Bodies and paths

Several manufacturers run their disputes through **BBB AUTO LINE** for Illinois
consumers; whether a manufacturer participates, and whether its process must be
used first, is stated in the warranty booklet. The Illinois Attorney General's
consumer-protection division takes complaints. Manufacturer "executive" and
repurchase-review channels are internal, not statutory — but they are where
complete records win without filings.

## Deadlines (rules and citations only — quote the source, ask who can confirm)

The Act's protection period and any arbitration filing windows are written in the
current statute and in the program's own rules. Quote those words, with the
citation and the date you read them, marked "not confirmed by anyone yet", and ask
legal aid, a courthouse self-help center, or a lawyer to confirm which applies to
you. Don't count days against either. General limitations periods for warranty
claims are a separate matter; counsel territory.

## Quirks

Days out of service are a fact about your repair orders, not something this file
counts. Repair-order open and close dates are the proof, so record who had the car
and why, continuously, including any time spent waiting for a part or a fix.
Whether and how any days count under the Act is a question for the statute's own
text and a lawyer.
